How 2026 went at Open Library: meeting readers where they are, earning their trust, and handing everyone the tools to build the library with us.

This is the 2026 story at Open Library, told through one idea: participation. Everything we built this year lowers the barrier between reading a book and improving the library. Three chapters: meeting readers where they are on mobile, earning their trust as a lending provider, and giving everyone the tools to contribute.

The spine of the talk is what's new this year, in three words: better UX, more ways to read, more ways to participate. First you meet readers where they already are, which in 2026 means mobile. Then you earn their trust by being a reliable place to actually borrow and read. Then, once they're here and they trust you, you hand them tools so the next improvement to the library comes from them, not just from us.

Chapter one: meet readers where they are. Most of our traffic is on a phone, so this year we rebuilt the core experience mobile-first. A new search UX that's fast and legible on a small screen. A new homepage, Browse the Stacks, that invites you to wander and discover instead of starting at an empty search box. And shelf actions: want-to-read, already-read, add-to-list, one tap from anywhere a book appears. Small taps, lower barrier.

New Search UX

One search box, two tabs: find a book, or search inside millions of them, with a Readable Only toggle so every result is one you can open right now.

Here's the new search. It opens from anywhere on the site, and it's built around two tabs. Books is what you'd expect. Inside books is the fun one: type a phrase like "it was the best of times" and we find it on the actual page, in books about typography, not just Dickens. We default to the exact phrase and give you one click to loosen it. Flip Readable Only and every hit is a book you can open this second. And it's thumb-first: big targets, filters up top, results you can scan on a phone.

Lenny: A Trusted Book Provider

Find a book, connect to Lenny for Libraries, and start reading. Borrowing is folded right into the reading flow.

  • Borrow in a tap. Authorize once, then read instantly in the browser.
  • A node you can trust. Lenny Labs runs lending as a dependable provider.
  • Read anywhere. Real loans with a due date, on web or phone.

Chapter two: earn trust. The best discovery in the world doesn't count if the borrow button is unreliable. Here's the whole flow on one book, Catboy: find it on Open Library, authorize Lenny for Libraries once, and you're reading in the browser, with a real loan and a real due date. That's what being a trusted book provider means. Trust is what turns a visitor into someone who stays, and someone who stays is who contributes.

Chapter three, the heart of it: hand people the tools. Four of them this year. I'll take each in turn: share snippets, the librarian tasks dashboard, community translations, and UI book tagging. Each one is deliberately small. Low friction is the whole strategy.

Share Snippets

Found a passage worth sharing? Highlight it right in BookReader and post it. No screenshots, no retyping.

  • Highlight → share. Select text in the reader and get a clean, linkable snippet.
  • A global feed. Every shared snippet joins a public feed of what people are reading.
  • A discovery loop. A snippet is an invitation that sends new readers back to the book.

Share snippets. If you've read something worth quoting, you shouldn't have to screenshot it. Highlight the passage in BookReader, share it, and you get a clean linkable snippet, which also flows into a global feed of what the community is reading. That feed is a discovery engine: every snippet points back at the book it came from.

Librarian /tasks

Improving the catalog used to mean knowing where to start. Now it's a to-do list anyone can pick from.

  • Bite-size micro-tasks. Well-scoped jobs: fix a cover, merge an author, add a date.
  • No onboarding. Grab a task, finish it in minutes, move on.
  • Real impact. Each one is a concrete improvement to a real record.

openlibrary.org/tasks

Today's micro-tasks

  • Add a cover for an edition missing one
  • Merge two duplicate author records
  • Add the publish date to a 2024 edition
  • Tag a book with its genre
The librarian tasks dashboard. The hardest part of contributing to a catalog is knowing where to start, so we turned it into a to-do list. Bite-size, well-scoped micro-tasks: fix a cover, merge a duplicate author, add a missing date. No onboarding, pick one up and finish it in minutes. Every task is a concrete improvement to a real record.

/translate

Open Library should read naturally in your language, and the community is what makes that happen.

  • One dashboard. A single place to translate and review interface strings.
  • Reviewed, then shipped. Language leads review contributions before they go live.
  • Already live. Translations across two dozen languages our readers speak.

openlibrary.org/translate

    Community translations. Open Library should read naturally in your language, and the community drives that through a single translations dashboard: translate a string, a language lead reviews it, and it ships. What you're watching on the right is the same book re-rendered in each language our community has translated. It's already live across roughly two dozen languages, and any reader can add theirs.
